<decisions_made>

- spu94_envelope_state_t.level is int32_t, not int16_t — unclamped intermediates overflow int16_t during attack steps (e.g. 43008 > 32767). Callers (ADSR, sweep) handle clamping after reading back.
- Phase bit ignored when direction=decrease AND mode=exponential — DuckStation interpretation, nocash says "seems to have no effect". Documented as LOW confidence in ADR-0059.
- Sweep modifies vol_l/vol_r directly — the volume register IS the sweep working state, not a separate multiplier. This matches PS1 hardware.

<context>
The shared envelope step helper was the key architectural move this phase. The PS1
uses one counter-accumulate circuit for both ADSR and sweep — extracting it into a
shared function guarantees bit-identical timing and prevents formula divergence.

The int16_t overflow bug was caught during testing — attack steps of 14336 from
level 28672 produce 43008 which wraps int16_t negative, creating an infinite loop.
Fixed by making the shared state use int32_t for level, letting callers clamp.
</context>
