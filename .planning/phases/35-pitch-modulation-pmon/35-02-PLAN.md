    ADR-0057 title: "VxOUTX capture point for PMON pitch modulation"
    Status: Accepted (2026-05-22, Phase 35)

    Context section must cover:
    - The nocash spec says PMON reads "prev voice amplitude" but does not specify which pipeline stage
    - Three possible capture points: post-Gauss (before ADSR), post-ADSR (before volume), post-volume (after L/R)
    - Each gives different modulation behavior: pre-ADSR ignores envelope, post-volume includes stereo pan

    Decision section must state:
    - SPU-94 captures VxOUTX at post-ADSR, pre-volume (Step 2.75 in voice_tick)
    - The value is v->outx = gauss_out after the ADSR multiply, before the vol_l/vol_r multiply
    - This matches DuckStation: voice.last_volume = ApplyVolume(sample, voice.regs.adsr_volume) -- this
      value is then used as the PMON factor for the next voice
    - Confidence: HIGH

    Also document in the same ADR:
    - PMON post-modulation pitch clamp: step clamped to 0x4000 (not the base pitch maximum of 0x3FFF).
      This is intentional per spec: "if Step > 3FFFh then Step = 4000h". FM modulation can push pitch
      beyond the normal register maximum. This means a PMON-modulated voice can play at slightly
      above the maximum base pitch.

    Consequences section:
    - Modulator voice ADSR directly controls FM depth (attack = increasing modulation, release = fading)
    - Volume sign/magnitude does NOT affect PMON factor (SVOL-04 confirmed in Phase 34)
    - PMON chain stacking reads each voice's post-ADSR output, not the final stereo output

    Sources: cite nocash psx-spx SPU ADPCM Pitch page, DuckStation spu.cpp ApplyVolume line,
    Phase 34 implementation (Step 2.75).
  </action>

    This test proves Phase 35 success criterion 1: "A modulator voice playing a slow sine
    sweeps the carrier voice's pitch audibly, with depth controlled by the modulator's ADSR."

    Test setup:
    - Voice 0 (modulator): plays a known ADPCM sample with a SLOW ADSR attack (attack_shift high,
      e.g., shift=10, step=0, exp=0 for a slow linear ramp). The ADSR starts at level 0 and
      ramps up gradually, so outx starts near zero and increases over many ticks.
    - Voice 1 (carrier): plays a known ADPCM sample with ADSR disabled or instant-max (shift=0,
      step=0, sustain_level=15, sustain_shift=31). PMON enabled on voice 1.
    - Both voices have the same base pitch (0x1000).

    Test logic:
    - Run the mixer for N ticks (e.g., 100). On each tick, record voices[1].pitch_counter.
    - In the early ticks (voice 0's ADSR is near zero), voice 1's effective pitch should be
      approximately half its base pitch (Factor near 0x8000).
    - In later ticks (voice 0's ADSR has ramped up, outx is larger), voice 1's effective pitch
      should be higher (Factor > 0x8000).
    - Specifically: sample the effective pitch step at tick 5 (early) and tick 80 (late).
      The late-tick pitch_counter delta should be LARGER than the early-tick delta, proving
      that the modulator's ADSR ramp increased the FM modulation depth.

    Register the test in main() under the "Phase 35: PMON" block, after the Plan 01 tests.
